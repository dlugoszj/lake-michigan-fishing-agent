# Agent design notes

Decisions made during extraction work that the agent (step 4) must implement.

- **Null catch quality is a weak positive, not bad fishing.** An observation with `catch_quality = null` means the species was found/caught but the report didn't say how well. When ranking ports, score it between 1 and 2 and say "quality not stated" in the answer. Extraction stays faithful to the text; this assumption lives here, where it's visible and tunable. (Decided 2026-10-17 on `39b4ba0-010`.)
- **Conditions = middle ground (2026-10-18).** Two numbers per port: measured buoy surface temperature + trend (NDBC), and a modeled cold-layer depth (NOAA LMHOFS: first 55 F, and the 52-58 F band, offshore at ~80/120/160 ft of water). Model surface was ~2 C off the buoys in a 7-point check, so depths are approximate and always labeled "modeled". Thresholds come from NY DEC (Chinook/coho 52-58 F) and Michigan Sea Grant (mature Chinook in 55 F or less; north/east winds cause upwelling). Upwelling flag = daily-mean surface drop >= 3 C within 2 days (heuristic).
- **Stored model profiles + pier point (2026-10-18).** `conditions/lake_model.py` stores a daily full profile per port at the pierhead cell and offshore at ~40/80/120/160 ft (table `lmhofs_profiles`, points in `lmhofs_points`), backfilled from March 2026, refreshed by the agent when the newest is >12 h old. Pier fishing is the user's main method: nearshore water is usually mixed top to bottom, so the agent sees pier surface/bottom temperature and its ~3-day change (upwelling signal). Points chosen once: nearest water cell to the harbor, and within 25 km the cell closest to each target depth. 2025 history would need the native `fields` files (sigma layers).
- **Later (deferred):** full temperature-at-depth profiles; historical offshore profiles (native-mesh files, ~4 GB); checking modeled cold-layer depth against the fish depths in DNR reports; wind/weather history per report week.
