Southeast Lower Peninsula

Lower Saginaw Bay: Fishing was slow with very anglers seen. There was reports of walleye caught on the east side of Spoils Island in 10 feet of water using crawlers.

Lake Erie: Yellow perch began schooling east of Stony Point. Some anglers caught fair numbers in approximately 18 feet of water before moving into deeper water. Perch rigs dressed with feathers and beads tipped with minnows were effective, with red producing the best results. Emerald shiners were in short supply, though other minnow species proved effective. Catches consisted of many small yellow perch, though larger fish did appear in the area. Walleye moved back across the state line into deeper waters on the Ohio side.

Lower Saginaw Bay: Boat anglers caught a few 9 to 11-inch yellow perch at sailboat buoys B and F, along with numerous 5 to 7-inch fish. Some boat anglers kept approximately 15 to 20 yellow perch per boat.

Eastern Saginaw Bay: Fishing was very slow across all access points over the past week. Water temperatures remained warm, with surface temperatures reported in the mid-70s. Anglers caught a few walleye near the Spark Plug midweek while trolling nightcrawler harnesses. Action remained slow overall, with cooler temperatures expected to improve fishing conditions.

Southwest Lower Peninsula

Muskegon: Boat anglers reported fair numbers of salmon 65 to 110 feet down in 90 to 150 feet of water. A white flasher and green fly combination was effective, along with glow spoons and plugs. Pier anglers were unsuccessful targeting salmon due to warm water temperatures.

Grand Haven: Salmon action was good for boat anglers 65 to 100 feet down in 85 to 140 feet of water. Green and white flies were effective. Pier anglers had success catching largemouth bass on drop-shot and Ned rigs. No salmon were reported from the pier by anglers casting spoons.

South Haven: Pier fishing was slow, though a few steelhead were caught by anglers casting spoons. Salmon anglers reported fair fishing, though rough lake conditions kept most boat anglers from reaching the most productive waters which were beyond 120 feet. A few salmon were caught inside 100 feet of water. Yellow perch anglers caught a few quality fish. Fishing remained inconsistent, depths around 45 feet of water had the most success.

St. Joseph: Pier fishing was slow for all species. Boat anglers targeting salmon caught fair numbers of fish, though rough weather conditions made it difficult for boat anglers to reach productive waters. The best bite occurred in around 120 feet of water. Yellow perch anglers reported good catches. Schools moved frequently, but depths around 40 feet of water were most productive.

Northeast Lower Peninsula

Tawas/Au Gres: Windy conditions kept many boat anglers off the water. When anglers were able to get out, fishing was slow overall. Anglers targeting walleye averaged 0 to 4 fish per boat, while those targeting yellow perch also returned with low catches. Shore anglers at the mouth of the Tawas River and at the state dock in Tawas caught primarily largemouth bass, smallmouth bass, and rock bass.

Alpena: A few walleye were caught in 20 to 30 feet of water while trolling nightcrawlers and deep-diving crankbaits near North Point and Sulphur Island. Anglers reported lake trout and a few Chinook salmon caught in 70 to 120 feet of water straight out of Thunder Bay. Flashers paired with Spin-N-Glos and spoons were productive fished near the bottom, with white, chartreuse, and blue patterns working best. A few Chinook salmon were also caught in the middle of the water column on spoons and plugs in green, black and green, and orange.

Thunder Bay River: A few Chinook salmon were caught near the Ninth Street Bridge on glow spoons during low-light conditions. Anglers reported slow fishing overall. A few smallmouth bass and northern pike were caught on leeches and small spinners.

Rockport: Walleye were caught in 20 to 40 feet of water near Middle Island. Anglers reported fair catches while trolling nightcrawlers and crankbaits. Lake trout fishing was very good for anglers targeting 80 to 120 feet of water. Flashers paired with Spin-N-Glos along with spoons in white, chartreuse, and watermelon produced the best results. A few Chinook salmon were caught near False Presque Isle in 60 to 90 feet of water. Plugs, spoons, and cut bait yielded mixed results early and late in the day.

Cheboygan: Chinook salmon were seen surfacing at the Cheboygan pier and the Cheboygan Dam spillway at daybreak and nightfall, though catch rates slowed. An occasional Chinook salmon was caught while trolling J-plugs and spoons between the buoy markers and the fish cleaning station. From the pier, salmon were caught periodically on small spoons. At the Cheboygan Dam spillway, anglers had success drifting spawn or casting spoons and minnow-style crankbaits. Small walleye, smallmouth bass, and rock bass were caught while drifting nightcrawlers between the Cheboygan Dam and the pedestrian walkway bridge.

Oscoda/Au Sable: Anglers targeting lake trout had good success in 120 to 140 feet of water while trolling spoons in the bottom 20 feet of the water column. Steelhead and a few chinook salmon were caught in 80 to 120 feet of water. Anglers fishing from the pier and catwalk had good success catching channel catfish using nightcrawlers on the bottom. A few walleye were caught by anglers casting deep-diving crankbaits at dusk.

Harrisville: Anglers targeting lake trout in 115 to 140 feet of water had good success on spoons and Spin-N-Glos. A few steelhead were also caught on spoons fished higher in the water column. Anglers casting spinners in the harbor caught a few northern pike.

Rogers City: Salmon were staging off Swan Bay, though the bite was inconsistent. Overall numbers remained low, with the best action occurring before sunrise, after sunset, or on overcast days. Productive depths ranged from 30 to 90 feet of water depending on the day, with fish moving shallower early in the morning and after dark. Trolling lines throughout the water column produced the best results. Anglers used standard and magnum spoons, J-plugs, and meat rigs. Flasher-fly combinations and squids also took fish. Productive colors included green, blue, chartreuse, orange, white, black, and UV patterns, with glow lures being effective early and late in the day. Walleye were caught as incidental catches. Anglers venturing outside the Swan Bay area caught lake trout, along with incidental steelhead and coho salmon. Lake trout were caught in colder water near the bottom, while steelhead and coho salmon were found higher in the water column.

Presque Isle: Fishing improved, though angling effort remained low. Anglers fishing between the lighthouses or south toward Stoneport caught a mixed bag of lake trout, along with an occasional salmon or walleye. Productive depths ranged from 45 to 90 feet of water, with lines spread throughout the water column producing the best results. Standard and magnum spoons caught fish, and anglers also ran flasher-fly and squid combinations. Productive colors included green, blue, orange, chartreuse, black, purple, white, and UV patterns, with effective glow patterns early and late in the day.

Northwest Lower Peninsula

Manistee: Anglers targeting Chinook salmon caught fish along the shelf in 90 to 200 feet of water while trolling 60 to 90-plus feet down with plugs, spoons, and flies. A few coho salmon and steelhead were also caught. Very few Chinook salmon were caught outside the pierheads in 35 feet of water while trolling plugs. Warm water temperatures kept pier fishing slow.

Ludington: Warm water remained in the area. Pier anglers experienced no salmon action over the weekend. Trolling boats had limited success off Big Sable Point when fishing in 135 to 200 feet of water, 70 to 90-plus feet down with plugs, spoons, flasher-fly combinations, and meat rigs. Salmon were also caught straight out in 85 to 180 feet of water while fishing 55 to 70-plus feet down. Boats heading south caught a few fish in 120 to 140 feet of water, 80 to 90 feet down. Catches consisted mostly of Chinook salmon, with a few coho salmon and steelhead mixed in.

Charlevoix: Boat anglers reported warm water temperatures, with the thermocline located 75 to 90 feet down with abundant baitfish in the area. Anglers fishing from the pier reported an occasional legal-size smallmouth bass caught on nightcrawlers and other natural bait.

Little Traverse Bay: Anglers fishing the Bear River reported a slow but steady run of salmon entering the river system. Boat anglers trolling the bay reported abundant baitfish scattered throughout the area. Water temperatures in the bay remained warm over the past week, with anglers locating the thermocline 75 to 85 feet down.

East Grand Traverse Bay: Anglers heading south of the Center Road launch caught both salmon and lake trout while trolling in 110 to 140 feet of water. Spoons, plugs, and flasher-fly combinations were all effective. Anglers trolling out of Elk Rapids in 100 to 160 feet of water caught lake trout, with a few chinook salmon mixed in. Water temperatures throughout the bay ranged from 70 to 73 degrees.

West Grand Traverse Bay: Lake trout were caught by anglers trolling in 120 to 160 feet of water along the west bank just north of Elmwood Marina. Anglers trolling near the mouth of the Boardman River also caught a few Chinook salmon, though action was slow due to warm water temperatures. Surface water temperatures ranged from 69 to 73 degrees throughout the bay.

Leland: Anglers targeting Chinook salmon reported fair catches. Successful boat anglers trolled north from the docks on North Manitou Island in depths of 150 to 300 feet of water, running lures 60 to 125 feet down. Green presentations, including plugs, spoons, and flies, were effective.

Anglers fishing around the Crib caught a few coho salmon. Fish moved closer to shore, trolling north along the first and second banks were successful for anglers targeting chinook salmon. Pier fishing was slow.

Frankfort/Lake Michigan: Chinook salmon were caught out deep on the bank in the early morning and closer to shore during the evening. Anglers reported good numbers caught on spoons and plugs. Anglers jigging in the harbor reported moderate success using jigging spoons and skein. Anglers fishing Platte Bay reported very good catches of coho salmon while drifting skein near the bottom in 40 to 80 feet of water.

Onekama/Lake Michigan: Anglers trolling in 180 to 220 feet of water in front of the golf course and the barrel reported a few Chinook salmon were caught on spoons in the early morning hours. Numbers slowed down as the warm water kept Chinook salmon in deeper water.

Northport/Suttons Bay: Anglers targeted Chinook salmon and lake trout. Anglers trolling north out of Suttons Bay along the shoreline toward Omena Bay caught a few Chinook salmon. Lake trout were caught while jigging near Stony Point and Omena Bay. Water temperatures were reported in the mid-60s.

Upper Peninsula

Little Bay de Noc: Walleye fishing was good, with anglers targeting reefs and nearby drop-offs. Trolling slowly near the bottom with crawler harnesses was the most effective method. Yellow perch anglers reported fair to good fishing, though action varied from day to day, with schools located in 20 feet of water or deeper. Coho salmon anglers reported a few fish in the river. Morning hours were the most productive, though the overall bite for coho salmon remained slow, with only a few boats recording productive trips while trolling standard-sized spoons or crankbaits.

Munising: Boat anglers reported good success jigging and trolling for lake trout around Grand Island and Wood Island. Salmon action was slow for trolling boat anglers, though a few splake were caught in the bay. Anglers fishing off the mouth of the Anna River reported slow splake action, with only a few fish caught. Spawn and spoons were the most effective presentations.

Ontonagon River: Angling effort on the river increased slightly over the past week and during the holiday weekend. Anglers caught walleye in low to fair numbers while jigging. Trolling was not an effective method for locating fish. Fishing was productive during the early morning hours.

Ontonagon/Silver City/Union Bay: Angling effort out of Ontonagon increased slightly over the past week. Effort from the Silver City and Union Bay access sites also increased, though it remained lower than that observed in Ontonagon. Anglers reported fair catches of lake trout with lower numbers of coho salmon. Successful boat anglers trolled in depths ranging from 50 to 150 feet of water.

Black River Harbor: The harbor saw a slight increase in angling effort over the holiday weekend. Anglers reported catches of brown trout, steelhead, coho salmon, and lake trout in low to fair numbers. Lake trout remained the most common catch. Anglers caught fish while trolling in depths ranging from 50 to 150 feet of water.

St. Ignace: Although fishing pressure increased at the Carp River, catch rates remained very low for Chinook salmon. The few successful anglers caught fish casting small glow spoons before daybreak and at nightfall.

Grand Marais: Boat anglers had good success catching lake trout while trolling and jigging. Anglers reported slow salmon action.

Manistique: Anglers reported fair fishing near the river mouth. A few salmon were reported in the river, though the primary run for salmon is yet to be seen. Anglers trolling in deeper water near the Garden Peninsula or south of Manistique reported fair catches of salmon. Most mature fish were seen moving toward river systems.

Marquette: Most anglers trolled north of White Rocks toward Granite Rock and the Clay Banks in 80 to 160 feet of water for lake trout. Anglers reported catching lake trout throughout the water column with no consistent pattern. A few anglers reported catching salmon and brown trout near Little Presque Isle, the Clay Banks, Granot Loma, and the Little Garlic River in 60 to 100 feet of water, though action was slow overall. Angling effort remained low off the Sand River and Shot Point, where lake trout numbers were low except in deeper water near the Sand Hole in approximately 180 feet of water.

Au Train: Angling effort out of Au Train increased substantially over the past week following low fishing pressure throughout the summer, with catch rates reflecting the improvement. On Saturday, many boat anglers caught bag limits of lake trout. Boat anglers targeted depths similar to previous weeks, working 140 to 180 feet of water along the Flats northeast of Au Train Island and west of the Wood Island Reef. All lake trout were caught near the bottom. Anglers targeting brown trout and salmon in shallower depths of 40 to 60 feet of water reported marking high concentrations of fish on sonar, though the fish did not bite.

Keweenaw Bay/Huron Bay: Anglers reported fair fishing, with catches consisting primarily of lake trout along with an occasional coho salmon or brown trout. Anglers jigging for lake trout reported a fair to poor bite, with fish taken on both natural and artificial presentations. Most catches occurred in approximately 150 feet of water, though some boats reported success in depths greater than 200 feet.

Lake Independence, Big Bay: Fishing activity increased, with most anglers targeting walleye and yellow perch. Anglers had the best success while trolling in 10 to 15 feet of water using natural bait.

Traverse Bay/Portage Entry: Anglers reported fair to good fishing when targeting lake trout while trolling spoons and flasher-fly combinations around structure in 50 to 100 feet of water. A few coho salmon were also caught while trolling nearshore waters or around river mouths. Anglers jigging for lake trout reported a fair bite using natural presentations, with most fish caught in 100 to 140 feet of water and some success reported in depths greater than 200 feet.

Fishing tip: Fall is an important time to think about gear maintenance

With the colder months quickly approaching, many anglers may be getting ready to store their gear for the season. Below are a few maintenance tips you should follow so your gear is cared for and ready to be used next season.

  1. Make sure your gear is clean and completely dry before storing it. Start by cleaning everything (rods, reels and line) in fresh water with soap or with the manufacturer’s recommended solution to remove any materials that may have become attached or embedded.
  2. Inspect your gear for any damage and make any repairs or prepare for replacements.
  3. Don’t store any of your gear in direct sunlight and don’t store any of your gear where heat and/or moisture might build up.
  4. Air your waders out completely and don’t forget to hang them upside down for the months they are out of use.
